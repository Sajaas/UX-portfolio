Case Study

BlackForge VR

I conducted multiple tutorial UX tests for our title BlackForge VR, a VR weaponsmithing simulator!

Since the onboarding is part of the gameplay, the information is spread out over a long time. The tutorial playtests had to be done in 2 sessions. For the first session I met the players in person, for the second one I recruited players from outside Sweden and conducted an online usability test.

IDENTIFYING THE ISSUES

I conducted tutorial usability tests, split in 2 playtests.

Top insights and suggestions from the testing include:

  • Feedback and quest loop was confusing
  • Order descriptions was sometimes lacking and/or confusing
  • Order feedback didn't make sense at times
  • The icons were unclear
  • The welding / dewelding action wasn't sufficiently explained
  • Players can get stuck because they don't follow the "correct" order
  • Correct placement to heat the ingot is unclear
  • Ore scoop is not working like an IRL scoop
  • Tongs-to-hand shortcut wasn't explained
  • Hammer felt inconsistant
  • Tutorial holograms can be misleading
SUGGESTED SOLUTIONS

  • Redesign how orders are turned in, such as separating it from the non-complete orders
  • Let someone new to the game proof read the order descriptions and feedback
  • Use standard icons
  • Either redesign how to weld/deweld or introduce it in more detail
  • Clarify the feedback when placing the ingot in the wrong area of the blackforge
  • Update how the scoop works, allowing player to tilt it forward to empty it
  • Introduce the tongs shortcut
  • Balance the hammer interaction (yeah, easier said than done)
  • Update tutorial holograms to only include what's relevant
KEY TAKEAWAYS
  • Extra stuff distracts, only include the essential information / image
  • Standardized language and icons quickly help players identify actions
  • Players have mental models of real-world-items, make sure it's use corresponds to it
  • Always get feedback on texts from someone new to the game
